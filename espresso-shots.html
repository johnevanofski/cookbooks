Coffee and espresso in grams (weigh the cup on a scale). Water in milliliters (mL), about 2 mL per gram of coffee is absorbed by the puck. 4 shots = two double baskets of 18 g, pulled back to back.

Shot Styles

Ristretto"Restricted" shot. Same coffee, about half the water of a standard shot (1:1.5). Short, thick, sweet and intense, with less bitterness.
EspressoThe standard shot (1:2). Balanced sweetness, acidity and body.
Lungo"Long" shot. Same coffee, more water (1:3). Larger, lighter body and thinner, with more bitterness if over-extracted.

Pull Steps

  1. Warm the portafilter and cup; wipe the basket dry.
  2. Weigh and grind the dose directly into the basket.
  3. Distribute evenly, then tamp level with firm, steady pressure.
  4. Place the cup on a scale, start the shot and the timer.
  5. Target 25–30 seconds from start to finish. Water ≈ 195–205°F, 9 bar.

Grind Size

FineFiner than table salt, close to powdered sugar. Starting point for espresso.
Too fast?Under 20 sec, watery or sour: grind finer.
Too slow?Over 35 sec, bitter or dry: grind coarser.

Change the grind in small steps and keep the dose the same.

Coffee Types

Medium-dark roastClassic espresso: chocolatey, full body, forgiving. Best starting point.
Dark roastBold, low acidity. Grind slightly coarser, cooler water.
Light roastBright, fruity. Grind finer, hotter water, a longer 1:2.5 ratio often helps.
Espresso blendMade for the machine; consistent and easy.

Use fresh beans (roasted within ~4 weeks; resting 7+ days after roast is best) and grind right before pulling.
